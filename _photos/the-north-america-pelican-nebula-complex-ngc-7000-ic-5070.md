Sprawling across the rich, star-studded background of the Milky Way in the constellation Cygnus, this magnificent wide-field composition captures one of the most iconic and recognizable star-forming complexes in the northern sky: NGC 7000 and its companion IC 5070, better known as the North America and Pelican Nebulae.

Dominating the scene on the left is the North America Nebula, whose striking geographic resemblance to the North American continent is uncanny. From the distinct peninsula of Florida and the Gulf of Mexico down to the dark, obscuring dust lanes that carve out the Atlantic coastline, the region glows with the rich, deep crimson light of ionized hydrogen gas.

Immediately adjacent to it, separated by a sweeping dark wall of cold molecular dust, is the Pelican Nebula (IC 5070). Its graceful, curved silhouette distinctly evokes a pelican with a long bill and outstretched pouch, dipping its head into the cosmic sea.

Though they appear as separate entities, both nebulae are actually part of the exact same massive interstellar hydrogen cloud, energized from behind by a yet-unidentified massive, hot star (likely located within the Cygnus OB2 association). Deep within these glowing clouds, intense radiation and stellar winds are actively sculpting pillars of dust, triggering the birth of thousands of hidden newborn stars.
