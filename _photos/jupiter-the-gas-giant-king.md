Dominating the planetary realm as the largest world in our Solar System, Jupiter is a turbulent, dynamic masterpiece of gas and high-speed wind. Composed almost entirely of hydrogen and helium, this gas giant rotates so rapidly (completing a day in just 9.9 hours) that it is significantly flattened at the poles, appearing slightly oblate through a telescope.

High-resolution planetary imaging reveals Jupiter’s complex, ever-shifting atmosphere in intricate detail. This portrait is defined by alternating light-colored zones (upwelling gas) and darker belts (descending gas), created by powerful zonal winds rushing at hundreds of kilometers per hour.

Visible across the disk are countless dynamic features: small, swirling white ovals, turbulent barges, complex storm systems, and the legendary Great Red Spot. This monstrous anticylonic storm, larger than the Earth itself, has raged in Jupiter’s southern hemisphere for centuries. The gas giant is often framed by its four brightest companions—Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto—the Galilean moons, which can be seen in transit across the cloud tops or silhouetted against the velvet blackness of space.
