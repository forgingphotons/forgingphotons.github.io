Dominating the sword of the celestial hunter, Messier 42 (the Orion Nebula) is perhaps the most famous, studied, and visually breathtaking stellar nursery in the entire night sky. Easily visible to the naked eye as a hazy patch in the constellation Orion, a telescopic or long-exposure view reveals a magnificent, turbulent sea of glowing gas, dark dust lanes, and newborn stars.

At the heart of M42 lies the Trapezium Cluster, a tight-knit family of massive, hot young stars whose intense ultraviolet radiation lights up the surrounding hydrogen gas, causing it to glow in brilliant shades of crimson, pink, and deep teal. The powerful stellar winds and radiation from these newborn giants have blown out a cavity in the center of the cloud, creating the stunning, cavernous "Huygens region" viewed here.

Beyond its striking beauty, the Orion Nebula serves as our primary laboratory for studying star birth. High-resolution imaging has revealed hundreds of protoplanetary disks (proplyds) scattered across the nebula—solar systems in the very act of forming around infant suns. M42 bridges the gap between deep-space majesty and the intimate genesis of planetary systems like our own.
