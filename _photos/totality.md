Captured during the extraordinary eclipse of August 12, 2026—the first total solar eclipse visible from mainland Europe in over a quarter of a century—this image records totality from the stark, arid landscapes near Zaragoza, Spain. Positioned in the dry interior of Aragón, where low-humidity desert air provided exceptional atmospheric clarity, the Moon moved precisely into alignment to completely obscure the solar disk low above the western horizon.   

Radiating into the darkened sky is the solar corona, a pearly-white halo of superheated plasma sculpted into delicate streams along the Sun's intricate magnetic field lines. Because the eclipse took place in late afternoon close to sunset, the low solar elevation coupled with the clear desert air granted the surrounding sky a deep twilight hue.   

Tracing the edge of the dark lunar silhouette are glowing, deep-pink solar prominences (or flares). These colossal loops of incandescent hydrogen gas burst thousands of kilometers above the Sun’s chromosphere, creating a striking contrast against the ghostly white corona and marking the brief, magical window of total dark before the Sun re-emerged.
