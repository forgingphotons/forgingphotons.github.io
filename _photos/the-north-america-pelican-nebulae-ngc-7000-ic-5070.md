Dominating a rich starfield in the constellation Cygnus, this iconic duo forms a massive interstellar cloud of ionized hydrogen, energized by hidden, extremely massive young stars. Though given two distinct names due to their striking visual forms, the North America Nebula (NGC 7000) and the Pelican Nebula (IC 5070) are actually two parts of the exact same sprawling cosmic cloud complex.

What appears to separate them into distinct shapes—a uncanny replica of the North American continent on the left and a long-billed bird on the right—is not empty space, but a dense, opaque foreground lane of interstellar dust known as LDN 935. This dark dust cloud acts as a cosmic curtain, blocking the glowing background gas and carving out the familiar contours of the Gulf of Mexico, Florida, and the Pelican's neck.

Deep within these glowing gas clouds lies an active cosmic nursery. The region corresponding to the "Gulf of Mexico" and the "Pelican's beak" is packed with dark globules, protostars, and energetic stellar jets (Herbig-Haro objects) where brand-new solar systems are actively coalescing out of dust and collapse under gravity.
