This striking deep-sky frame captures a fascinating cosmic juxtaposition in the constellation Ursa Major, bringing together two vastly different celestial evolutionary endpoints lying side by side in the same patch of sky: a dying star's glowing ghost and a distant island universe.

On the left is Messier 97 (The Owl Nebula), one of the most complex and famous planetary nebulae in the night sky. Roughly 2,000 years ago, a Sun-like star exhausted its nuclear fuel and gently sloughed off its outer layers, leaving behind a hot, dying white dwarf at its core. The expanding shell of gas forms a faint, circular green-hued bubble. Deep visual and photographic analysis reveals two dark, shadowy cavities within the glowing gas—resembling the ghostly eyes of an owl peering out into the dark.

In sharp contrast, lurking in the background on the right is Messier 108 (The Surfboard Galaxy). Seen almost completely edge-on from our perspective, M108 is a sprawling spiral galaxy located vastly farther away than the Owl Nebula. Instead of a smooth disc, its slender profile reveals a mottled, chaotic texture of dark dust lanes, glowing knots of active star formation, and young blue clusters scattered across its starry expanse.

Though they appear close neighbors from our line of sight on Earth, M97 and M108 are separated by millions of light-years—offering a stunning visual bridge between our local stellar neighborhood and the vast depths of extragalactic space.
