Reaching out from the rich star fields of the constellation Cassiopeia, NGC 281—widely known as the Pacman Nebula—is a vibrant emission nebula and active stellar nursery. Its popular moniker comes from its distinctive, rounded shape and a prominent dark lane carved into the glowing gas, giving it a striking resemblance to the classic arcade video game character about to chomp down on passing stars.

Sprawling across nearly a hundred light-years of space, the nebula glows with the rich crimson light of hydrogen gas ionized by ultraviolet radiation. At its center lies IC 1590, a brilliant, young open cluster of massive stars whose fierce stellar winds and powerful radiation pressure are actively shaping the region. These stellar winds sweep away surrounding gas and carve out deep hollows in the molecular cloud, exposing dense, shadowy knots known as Bok globules. Within these isolated pockets of dust and cold gas, gravity continues to pull material inward, quietly nurturing the next generation of embryonic stars.
