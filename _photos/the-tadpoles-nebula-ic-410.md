Sprawling across the rich winter Milky Way in the constellation Auriga, IC 410 is a luminous and complex emission nebula powered by a brilliant embedded star cluster. This vast cloud of glowing interstellar gas and dust serves as a grand laboratory for stellar birth and high-energy radiation physics.

At the heart of the nebula lies NGC 1893, a young open cluster consisting of thousands of massive, hot stars born from the surrounding material only a few million years ago. These stellar heavyweights blast out intense ultraviolet radiation and fierce stellar winds, lighting up the encompassing hydrogen gas in a deep, sweeping crimson glow and carving out a glowing cavity in the center of the cloud.

The most famous features of IC 410 are its two remarkable "tadpoles"—dense, twisting structures of dust and gas that stream away from the central cluster. Stretching for roughly 10 light-years, these cosmic tadpoles consist of head-like reservoirs of cooler gas with long, sweeping tails pointing directly away from the ionizing radiation of the central stars. Sculpted by the harsh stellar winds, these dense pillars are sites where dust and gas are compressed, potentially shielding embryonic protostars as they struggle to form against the surrounding cosmic tempest.
