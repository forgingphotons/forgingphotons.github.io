Discovered in March 2020 by NASA's NEOWISE space telescope, Comet C/2020 F3 (NEOWISE) became one of the most spectacular astronomical events of the 21st century. As a rare "great comet," it grew bright enough to be easily seen with the naked eye, enchanting stargazers and astrophotographers worldwide during the quiet mid-2020 summer nights.

What made NEOWISE particularly stunning in deep exposures was its magnificent dual tail structure stretching millions of kilometers into space. The prominent, glowing golden tail was composed of tiny dust particles pushed away by the pressure of sunlight, trailing gracefully along the comet's orbit. Beside it sat a fainter, straight blue ion tail, formed by charged gas molecules stripped away directly by the high-speed solar wind.

Hailing from the frozen, distant reaches of the Oort Cloud, NEOWISE survived a scorching close pass by the Sun (*perihelion*) without breaking apart. Its orbital path was heavily altered by the Sun's gravity during this visit—extending its orbital period so that it won't return to the inner Solar System for another 6,800 years.
