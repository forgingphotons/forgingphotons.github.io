Captured during its historic inner-solar system voyage, this striking wide-field image preserves a rare celestial meeting between the famous green visitor Comet C/2022 E3 (ZTF) and the rust-red planet Mars. Sweeping past the background stars of Taurus in mid-February 2023, the comet approached within roughly 1.5 degrees of the Red Planet, creating a vivid color contrast that thrilled astrophotographers worldwide.

Comet C/2022 E3 (ZTF)—discovered by the Zwicky Transient Facility—made headlines as it completed an orbit that last brought it near our planet during the Upper Paleolithic era, some 50,000 years ago. In this frame, the comet’s characteristic emerald-green coma glows brightly, energized by the fluorescence of diatomic carbon molecules ($\text{C}_2$) interacting with sunlight. Faint, intricate dust and ion tails stream backward, sculpted by the solar wind.

Just a short celestial hop away, Mars shines with a fierce, warm amber hue, providing a stunning visual counterpoint to the ethereal, icy visitor from the distant Oort cloud. This dynamic pairing brings together two entirely different worlds of our solar system in a single frame—a fleeting moment frozen in time against the timeless backdrop of the cosmos.
