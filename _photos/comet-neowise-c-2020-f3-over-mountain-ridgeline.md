Captured during the warm twilight of July 2020, this evocative nightscape captures the majestic passage of Comet C/2020 F3 (NEOWISE) as it swept low over the jagged silhouette of a mountain range. Discovered by NASA’s Near-Earth Object Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer in March 2020, NEOWISE rapidly grew into the brightest naked-eye comet visible from the Northern Hemisphere since Comet Hale-Bopp in 1997.

Framed against the soft gradient of dusk, the comet’s brilliant, glowing nucleus shines just above the dark mountain ridges. Expanding outward into space are its two distinct tails: a broad, gently curved dust tail formed as solar radiation pressure pushes away microscopic grains of ancient dust, alongside a faint, straight, electric-blue ion tail ionized directly by the energetic solar wind.

This composition balances the timeless, grounded stillness of the terrestrial landscape with the dynamic motion of a distant icy wanderer. Sweeping in on an elliptical orbit lasting nearly 6,800 years, NEOWISE offers a fleeting, unforgettable reminder of the ancient, primitive material preserved at the cold outer edges of our solar system.
