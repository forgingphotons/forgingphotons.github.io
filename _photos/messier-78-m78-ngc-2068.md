Nestled within the vast stellar nursery complex of the constellation Orion, Messier 78 is the brightest diffuse reflection nebula in the night sky. Rather than glowing from its own ionized gas like emission nebulae, M78 shines with a ghostly, ethereal blue light because its dense microscopic dust grains reflect and scatter the intense ultraviolet radiation of hot, young, embedded stars.

Discovered in 1780 by Pierre Méchain and subsequently cataloged by Charles Messier, this cosmic cloud is dominated by a pair of bright B-type young stars (HD 38563A and HD 38563B) that illuminate the surrounding labyrinth of dark lanes and swirling dusty tendrils.

Beneath its serene, icy-blue exterior, M78 is an active site of ongoing star birth. Deeply embedded within the obscuring molecular dust clouds are numerous infrared protostars—infant suns still in the earliest stages of formation, firing high-speed jets of gas into their surrounding cocoons and offering a fascinating glimpse into the turbulent genesis of planetary systems.
