Sprawling across a vast patch of sky nearly three degrees in diameter—roughly six times the width of the full Moon—the Cygnus Loop (also known as the Veil Nebula Complex) is the expanding shell of an ancient cosmic explosion. Captured here in a meticulous 9-panel Hydrogen-alpha (Ha) monochromatic mosaic, this panoramic view brings together the entire complex, from the Western Veil (NGC 6960, "The Witch's Broom") to the Eastern Veil (NGC 6992/6995) and Pickering's Triangle, in staggering continuous detail.

This massive ring of glowing filaments was born roughly 10,000 to 20,000 years ago when a star roughly 20 times more massive than our Sun reached the end of its life and exploded in a catastrophic supernova. The supersonic shockwave from that blast continues to plow through the interstellar medium at hundreds of kilometers per second, compressing and heating the surrounding hydrogen gas until it glows.

By isolating the narrow Hydrogen-alpha wavelength across all nine stitched panels, this high-resolution black-and-white presentation eliminates light scattering and highlights the intricate, thread-like shock fronts, delicate ripples, and cavernous geometry of the blast wave. It stands as a profound portrait of cosmic feedback—where the violent death of an ancient star scatters heavy elements back into space to seed the next generation of solar systems.
