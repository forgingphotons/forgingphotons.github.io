The Eagle Nebula (Messier 16) is a majestic stellar nursery where raw cosmic gas and dust are actively forged into new generations of stars. Captured in exquisite detail, the nebula gets its name from its sweeping, winged appearance that evokes a giant raptor soaring through a sea of glowing hydrogen gas.

At the heart of M16 lies one of the most famous and iconic images in the history of astronomy: the "Pillars of Creation." Imbued with a breathtaking, three-dimensional depth, these towering columns of cold molecular hydrogen and dust stretch light-years into space. They are simultaneously being sculpted and eroded by the fierce ultraviolet radiation and powerful stellar winds of a massive cluster of newborn blue stars (NGC 6611) located just above them.

Buried deep within these dark, dusty pillars are embryonic stars—known as evaporating gaseous globules, or EGGs—where gravity is currently collapsing material to ignite brand-new suns. M16 stands as a magnificent testament to the ongoing cycle of cosmic creation, where the energetic death and birth of stars play out side by side.
