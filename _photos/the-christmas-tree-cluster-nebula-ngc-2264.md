Blending festive wonder with intense cosmic physics, NGC 2264—widely known as the Christmas Tree Nebula—is a spectacular star-forming region in the constellation Monoceros. Captured in vibrant detail, this complex spans tens of light-years and features a triangular arrangement of glowing gas and brilliant stars that uncannily resembles a holiday pine tree suspended in space.

The "tree" itself is an open cluster of young, hot stars (NGC 2264) whose stellar winds and intense ultraviolet radiation illuminate and sculpt the surrounding interstellar material. The brilliant blue star S Monocerotis acts as the sparkling jewel near the center of the tree. The surrounding gas glows with the deep, rich crimson of ionized hydrogen, mixed with delicate regions of bluish reflection dust where starlight scatters off microscopic particles.

At the apex of the triangular structure (pointing toward the top of the "tree") lies the famous Cone Nebula, a dark, towering pillar of cold molecular hydrogen and dust stretching light-years into space. Carved away by the energetic winds of newborn massive stars, this dense column is an active stellar nursery where hidden protostars continue to collapse and ignite, painting a vivid portrait of cosmic birth and evolution.
