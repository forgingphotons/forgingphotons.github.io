Shining like a dense vault of celestial jewels against the dark background of space, Messier 13 (M13)—often hailed as the *Great Globular Cluster in Hercules*—is one of the brightest, oldest, and most famous star clusters visible from the Northern Hemisphere. Bound together by relentless mutual gravity, roughly hundreds of thousands of stars are packed tightly into a sphere measuring about 145 light-years across.

At the core of M13, stellar density is extraordinary: stars are packed hundreds of times more densely than in our local neighborhood of the Milky Way. In fact, if Earth were located near its center, the night sky would be permanently illuminated by thousands of brilliant suns. Within this dense stellar metropolis, stars frequently pass close to one another, giving rise to rare "blue stragglers"—stars that collide or transfer mass, rejuvenated into looking younger and bluer than their ancient peers.

M13 also holds a famous place in the history of human communication with potential extraterrestrial life. In 1974, the powerful Arecibo radio telescope beamed a famous encoded broadcast—the Arecibo Message—directly toward M13. Containing basic information about humanity, Earth, and DNA, this message is currently travelling through space and is scheduled to reach the cluster in about 22,000 years!
